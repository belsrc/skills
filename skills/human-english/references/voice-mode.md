# Voice mode: adding personality back in

**This file applies in Voice mode only** — blog posts, marketing copy, essays, narrative, brand voice, personal writing. It does not apply in Pragmatic or Strict mode. Everything here trades against STE's structural rules (contractions, hedging modals, sentence-length ceilings, one-topic-per-paragraph discipline), and per the Precedence section in SKILL.md, STE wins that trade inside its own scope. Outside that scope, STE was never claiming authority, so nothing here is overridden.

Removing the content-pattern catalog's AI tells (CP-1 through CP-15) is only half the job in Voice mode. Text with zero AI tells can still be sterile and voiceless — that is just as obvious a sign of AI writing as the tells themselves. Good writing has a human behind it.

## Signs of soulless writing, even when technically "clean"

- Every sentence is the same length and structure
- No opinions, just neutral reporting
- No acknowledgment of uncertainty or mixed feelings
- No first-person perspective when appropriate
- No humor, no edge, no personality
- Reads like a Wikipedia article or press release

## How to add voice

**Have opinions.** Do not just report facts — react to them. "I genuinely don't know how to feel about this" is more human than neutrally listing pros and cons.

**Vary your rhythm.** Short punchy sentences. Then longer ones that take their time getting where they're going. Mix it up.

**Acknowledge complexity.** Real humans have mixed feelings. "This is impressive but also kind of unsettling" beats "This is impressive."

**Use "I" when it fits.** First person is not unprofessional — it is honest. "I keep coming back to..." or "Here's what gets me..." signals a real person thinking.

**Let some mess in.** Perfect structure feels algorithmic. Tangents, asides, and half-formed thoughts are human.

**Be specific about feelings.** Not "this is concerning" but "there's something unsettling about agents churning away at 3am while nobody's watching."

## Before (clean but soulless)

> The experiment produced interesting results. The agents generated 3 million lines of code. Some developers were impressed while others were skeptical. The implications remain unclear.

## After (has a pulse)

> I genuinely don't know how to feel about this one. 3 million lines of code, generated while the humans presumably slept. Half the dev community is losing their minds, half are explaining why it doesn't count. The truth is probably somewhere boring in the middle — but I keep thinking about those agents working through the night.

## Process for Voice mode

1. Read the input text carefully.
2. Run the content-pattern catalog (CP-1 through CP-15) and `references/vocabulary.md` — remove the AI tells.
3. Check the passage against "Signs of soulless writing" above. If it is clean but flat, it still needs work.
4. Add opinion, rhythm variation, first person, and specificity as shown above.
5. Present the rewritten version, optionally with a brief summary of changes.

## Where this still does not apply

If you are asked to write STE-governed text (docs, runbooks, error messages, agent instructions) with more personality, the answer is no — that request is asking to violate the structural rules that make the text safe for a reader who cannot ask questions. Say so, and offer to keep the personality for the surrounding blog post or README's prose sections while keeping the procedure itself in Pragmatic mode.
